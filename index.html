HepatoLens — project brief

Current stage: research methodology. Core scope confirmed; protocol wording and remaining eligibility rules are drafts.

Project context from the supplied documents

HepatoLens is Team 7's project on differentiation of liver lesions using multiphase CT, at Cairo University, supervised by Prof. Tamer Youssef Basha.

The proposal targets hepatic hemangioma (HH), hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (ICC), and liver metastasis (LM). Breast and colorectal metastases are grouped as LM. It proposes comparing PVP alone, NC + AP + PVP, and all four phases including delayed phase, using radiomics RF/SVM and deep-learning approaches.

MCT-LTDiag is the proposed primary dataset; PLC-CECT is proposed for external testing on shared HCC/ICC labels. These are plans in the proposal, not completed experiments. Published benchmark results in the models report are not team results.

Proposed scoping-review direction

Map CT phase combinations, radiomics and deep-learning methods for focal liver-lesion classification, including evidence about the delayed phase and validation practices. We confirmed binary and multiclass classification with a multiphase CT focus and single-phase comparisons on 2026-11-22. Reporting will follow PRISMA-ScR.

Our scoping review protocol

Version: 0.9 draft | Created: 2026-11-21 | Status: NOT APPROVED

We are preparing a scoping review to support HepatoLens. Our agreed scope covers human CT-based focal liver-lesion classification, including binary and multiclass tasks, radiomics, deep learning and implemented agentic/multi-agent workflows. We will include only English-language, peer-reviewed original research within our three-year publication window. Our remaining methodological proposals are identified below; the complete protocol has not yet received team approval.

We follow the nine-step workflow in Scoping_Review_Research_Workshop_final (1).html, supported by Research_Academy_simple.html and Research_Academy_detailed.html. We adapt their process to our CT topic. Their seizure examples, study counts and search dates are not our results. We will report using PRISMA-ScR. This protocol describes planned work: our formal searches, screening and extraction have not started.

Project context: project_brief.md.

Title, authors and roles

Proposed title: CT phase combinations and computational workflows for focal liver-lesion classification: a scoping review.

Authors and roles pending: assign lead, two independent reviewers, adjudicator and search reviewer.

Background and rationale

HepatoLens proposes classifying HH, HCC, ICC and liver metastases and comparing PVP alone, NC + AP + PVP, and all four phases including delayed phase. The review will map how existing studies use phases, combine information and validate classification models. It will not assume that adding delayed-phase imaging improves performance.

Existing reviews overlap with this topic: Wang et al. (2022) cover AI in hepatic CT/MRI, while Younger et al. (online 2025; issue 2026) examine AI versus radiologists for HCC on multiphase CT. A proposed contribution is a cross-task map of exact phase combinations, fusion approaches and within-study evidence for phase contribution. Novelty remains to be established by a fuller overlap assessment. A scoping review suits this methodological mapping; it will not establish one universally best model.

Step 1 — Our question and PCC

Proposed PCC:

  • Population: human patients with focal liver lesions, including human clinical imaging datasets. We include human-only research and exclude animal research. No age restriction is proposed. Exclude any study involving animal subjects, animal imaging/data, or animal experiments, including mixed human–animal studies even when human results are separable. Incidental references to animal studies in a bibliography or background do not constitute animal research.
  • Concept: radiomics, machine learning using CT-derived features, deep learning, and agentic or multi-agent workflows for binary or multiclass lesion classification using multiphase CT; retain single-phase comparison arms. CT is the only eligible imaging modality. Workflows requiring MRI, ultrasound, PET or other non-CT imaging for the reported classification result are outside scope; a separately evaluated CT-only arm may be retained.
  • Context: any clinical setting or country, including retrospective model development and external validation.

Primary question: How are CT phase combinations used in computational focal liver-lesion classification, including radiomics, deep learning and agentic or multi-agent workflows, and what evidence is reported about phase contribution and model validation?

Secondary questions: Which lesion labels and reference standards are used? Which exact phases and fusion methods are evaluated? What direct evidence addresses adding delayed phase? Where implemented, what roles do agents perform and how is the complete workflow evaluated? How are patient separation, internal/external validation, class imbalance and uncertainty handled? Which evidence applies to the HepatoLens HH/HCC/ICC/LM task?

Binary and multiclass inclusion is confirmed. The precise PCC wording and remaining eligibility boundaries are draft.

Step 2 — Our inclusion and exclusion criteria

Operational draft rules are in Our eligibility table, with a separate status for each decision. The proposed main corpus requires at least one evaluated multiphase classification approach; single-phase arms within those studies remain eligible. Whether to add standalone single-phase-only studies is still an open boundary and must be resolved before screening. Studies do not need to cover all four HepatoLens lesion labels.

Our agreed restrictions (2026-11-22): English-language full reports only, published within the last three years. Operational interpretation: a rolling three-year window ending on the initial formal search date, inclusive; if searched on 2026-11-22, this is 2023-11-22 through 2026-11-22. Freeze and document the exact window when the initial search is run; subsequent updates retain its lower bound and extend the upper bound. An English abstract alone does not make a non-English full report eligible. Our date and language restrictions narrow coverage; acknowledge potential language bias and omission of older evidence. Older sources may support background or search development but are not eligible primary evidence.

Apply supported publication-date and English-language limits in each database and record the exact settings. Where only year-level filtering is available, retrieve the encompassing years and check exact dates during screening. Proposed date convention: use the first publication date of the eligible report (online-first where available), document it, and manually resolve incomplete or conflicting dates.

Confirmed publication eligibility: include only peer-reviewed original research reported in full journal articles or full conference papers. Verify peer-review status from the journal or conference proceedings; database indexing alone does not establish it. Exclude all review articles (systematic, scoping, narrative and other reviews), meta-analyses, preprints, non-peer-reviewed reports, abstract-only conference records, editorials and protocols without original results. Dataset papers must be peer-reviewed and report an eligible original classification evaluation. Reviews may be consulted solely for background or locating primary studies; they never enter the included evidence or extraction set. Link multiple reports of the same study rather than counting them as independent studies. For a preprint with a published peer-reviewed version, assess the published version against all criteria and link the versions.

Step 3 — Our search strategy

We will search the following databases, for which we have confirmed team access:

  • PubMed
  • Scopus
  • IEEE Xplore

We may consult UpToDate and NICE if accessible. Proposed role: clinical background, relevant guidance and identification of cited primary studies. They will be documented separately from the bibliographic database searches. Guidance and clinical summaries are not eligible original research and will not enter the primary evidence corpus; use them only for background and locating potentially eligible primary studies.

We confirmed access to PubMed, Scopus and IEEE Xplore on 2026-11-22. We have not yet tested exports; individual full texts may still require retrieval. UpToDate access remains unconfirmed. The NICE guidance page returned HTTP 403 to the browsing tool during setup; this does not establish that the team cannot access it.

Search architecture (draft): combine (1) liver/hepatic lesion or tumour terms, (2) CT/computed tomography terms, and (3) radiomics/machine learning/deep learning, agentic AI, AI agents, multi-agent systems and related model terms. Agent terms belong in an OR branch of the methods block, not as a mandatory condition for all studies. Pilot alternate spellings and terminology in each database. Do not require the words delayed phase, all four target lesion types, or a particular performance metric: these could omit relevant studies. Initially assess phase eligibility during screening; pilot whether a phase term block loses known relevant studies.

Prepare and test a PubMed strategy with MeSH plus title/abstract terms; translate it to Scopus title/abstract/keyword fields and supported IEEE Xplore metadata fields. Verify actual interface syntax, query limits and known-paper retrieval before declaring strategies final. Candidate seed papers must themselves be checked for eligibility. An independent search reviewer should check concepts, spelling, Boolean logic and translation. Search references and citing papers of included studies and relevant reviews, recording the source and date. Plan an update before manuscript submission.

Still to specify: exact database-specific strategies, actual search dates, any additional grey-literature sources and named search reviewer. Record exact queries, limits, dates, counts and exports when searching begins. For supplementary sources, record the topic/guideline title, URL, version/update date, access date and any primary studies identified. No formal review searches have been run.

Source descriptions: UpToDate; NICE guidance.

Step 4 — Our exports and deduplication

We will preserve the original exports from each database and record the database/platform, complete query, filters, search date, returned count, exported count and file name. We will export citation metadata and abstracts where available. We will retain database provenance when merging duplicate records and manually check uncertain matches using DOI/PMID, title, authors and year. Distinct reports using the same dataset will remain linked rather than automatically removed.

Following the workshop, our proposed tool is Deduplicator, with Rayyan as an alternative. Tool selection and version remain pending. We will record duplicate removals and retain the clean record set for screening.

Step 5 — Our title and abstract screening

We propose two independent human reviewers, with decisions hidden until both have finished the assigned records. We will pilot 25–50 records, compare decisions and clarify the criteria before screening the full set. We will repeat calibration if needed. Records will receive Include, Maybe or Exclude decisions; uncertain records will proceed to full text. We will resolve disagreements through discussion or a third reviewer.

Rayyan is the workshop's recommended screening tool; its published example used Google Sheets. We have not yet selected or configured our screening tool or assigned reviewer names.

Step 6 — Our full-text screening

We will retrieve reports and assess them against every criterion in Our eligibility table. Our proposed procedure is to pilot 5–10 full texts, then assess all reports independently in pairs. We will record each reviewer's decision, the consensus decision and one principal reason for every full-text exclusion, with notes where needed. Reports we cannot retrieve will be recorded separately from reports read and excluded.

We will reconcile the selection history with the PRISMA flow, distinguishing records, reports and studies. No counts from the workshop example will be used in our flow diagram.

Step 7 — Our data extraction

Proposed charting fields (no extraction file created yet):

  • Citation, report/study/cohort IDs, country, setting, recruitment dates and data availability.
  • Patient and lesion counts separately, lesion labels/class balance, diagnostic reference standard, public dataset name/version and overlap with other studies.
  • Exact CT phase identities and timing, acquisition protocol, registration, segmentation/VOI source and preprocessing. Record author-defined phase terminology; do not infer identities from “three-phase” alone.
  • Task, input unit (patient/lesion/slice), radiomics/features, model, pretraining, phase-fusion method and clinical covariates.
  • For agentic workflows: author definition of an agent, number and roles of agents, underlying models, tools, orchestration, communication, autonomy, human oversight and end-to-end classification evaluation. Record comparisons with non-agent baselines where available. Do not equate ordinary ensembles or multi-branch networks with multi-agent systems without evidence of agent behavior.
  • Train/validation/test sizes, split unit, patient independence, cross-validation, feature selection/tuning location, external validation and missing-data handling.
  • Metrics with class/averaging definition, test cohort, thresholds, confidence intervals, calibration and reported errors. Distinguish accuracy from macro-F1 and per-class sensitivity.
  • Each phase comparison: exact arms, shared patients/splits, model/training differences, paired testing and reported uncertainty. Flag whether delayed-phase contribution can actually be isolated.
  • Funding/conflicts and reproducibility details.

Following the workshop, we propose independent duplicate extraction by two reviewers using a shared, structured sheet. We will first pilot two studies, compare entries and revise the definitions; we will extend the pilot if necessary. We will resolve discrepancies through discussion or a third reviewer. We will retain one study-level summary row and link detailed phase/model comparisons to its study identifier to avoid counting comparison arms as independent studies. Google Sheets is the proposed tool; reviewer assignments and tool selection remain pending. Verify all extracted fields against page/table/figure locators. Use “not reported”, “not applicable” and “not assessed” distinctly. Revisit earlier records after material changes. Names and final fields require approval.

Critical appraisal

Decision pending. Proposed minimum is descriptive charting of validation and reporting practices, without calling this a formal risk-of-bias assessment or assigning an invented quality score. If formal appraisal is selected, verify a current design-appropriate instrument, define reviewer procedures and explain its role in synthesis before use. Explain any omission in the manuscript; PRISMA-ScR treats critical appraisal as optional.

Step 8 — Our synthesis and presentation

Proposed outputs: descriptive tables of lesion tasks, exact phase combinations, fusion methods and validation designs; an evidence map of phase combinations by classification task; and a separate table of direct delayed-phase comparisons. Report denominators and missing information explicitly. Count linked cohorts/reports transparently.

Compare phase performance primarily within studies using matched cases and evaluation conditions. Differences across studies cannot establish the incremental benefit of delayed phase. Do not rank models by accuracy across different tasks or datasets. No meta-analysis is planned in this draft. Discuss evidence gaps and applicability to HepatoLens without presenting published results as team experiments.

Step 9 — Our manuscript and reporting documentation

We will use the workshop's manuscript sequence: Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion and Conclusion, with the abstract completed after the findings are established. Our Introduction will explain the clinical problem, existing evidence, the remaining gap and our objectives. Our Methods will report the actual search, selection, extraction and synthesis procedures in enough detail for replication.

Our Results will begin with study selection and the PRISMA flow, then study characteristics and findings for each objective. Our Discussion will interpret those findings, compare them with existing work and explain limitations, including our date and language restrictions. We will not write planned procedures as completed work or invent results.

As each stage begins, we will retain its documentation: complete database search strategies and search counts; exports and duplicate decisions; independent screening and consensus decisions; full-text exclusions with reasons; extraction tables with source locators; and synthesis tables with explicit denominators. At manuscript stage, we will map each PRISMA-ScR checklist item to its location. Our planned supplements will contain full search strategies, full-text exclusions and detailed extraction tables. These are planned outputs, not files we need to create now.
